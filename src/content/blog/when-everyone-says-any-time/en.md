---
title: "When Everyone Says “Any Time Works”: How to Set a Group Date"
description: "Turn a vague group chat into a concrete plan with clear options and room to say no."
---

“Any time works for me.” After three friends say it, someone opens a calendar. Then comes the hard part: nobody knows exactly which times the others mean. This fictional group chat illustrates a familiar problem—and a few ways to move it forward.

## Offer choices people can actually answer

Instead of asking “When are you free?”, suggest dates, start times and an expected duration. Try: “Could you do two hours from 2 p.m. Saturday or from 11 a.m. Sunday?” If someone has a long journey, include the venue or at least the area.

## Make “neither works” a valid answer

Let people suggest another time or decline if both options are difficult. Check first with anyone whose attendance is essential, and find new options if none of the proposed times works. Nobody needs to be pressured into explaining why they cannot attend.

## Decide and share the plan

Say when you will collect responses, then share the chosen time and place. For example: “Let me know by Thursday evening—and please say so if neither works!” Clear options and an easy way to decline are a better starting point than guessing what everyone means by ‘any time’.
